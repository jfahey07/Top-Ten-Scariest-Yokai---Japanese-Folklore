The Top Ten Scariest Japanese Yokai

Yokai -- which can translate to demon, monster, spirit or goblin -- are a diverse range of supernatural enteties that include gods, ghosts, animals, and more. Despite the connotations of the translations, these spirits are not always evil. They can be benevolent, mischevious, and even omens of good fortune. The ones that are evil however, are downright terrifying. This list is dedicated to the top ten scariest Yokai that I've found on my search through Japanese folklore. Buckle up and put on your brown pants, these Yokai don't play when it comes to human encouonters.

  1. Mikoshi Nyudo
  2. Ushi Oni
  3. Umi Nyobo
  4. Teke Teke
  5. Obariyon
  6. Jorogumo
  7. Kotengu
  8. Kuro Bozu
  9. Tsurube Otoshi
  10. Tenome

1. Mikoshi Nyudo

"The Anticipating Priest" loves to stay near bridges and dark empty streets waiting for lonesome travelers. At first, the Mikoshi Nyudo looks like a harmless, wandering monk. But the moment you approach it and look up to meet its eyes, it springs its trap. The “monk” transforms instantly, sprouting talons, sharp teeth, and wild, beast-like hair while his neck elongates to an astounding height. As your eyes look up to meet his gaze, his neck increasingly elongates to whatever height your eyes can reach. His neck grows taller and taller, forcing your head to crook back further and further until you flip violently onto your back. The second you hit the ground, he swings his massive head back down and bites out your throat. Some people are so paralyzed by the sheer terror of the transformation that they die on the spot before Mikoshi Nyudo even touches them. If you try to run or look away, it will grow furious and crush you or impale you with bamboo spears. The only escape is to look it confidently in the eyes, then look from its head straight down to its feet and confidently say, “You have lost, I know your tricks.” Frustrated, the monster will vanish into the night.

2. Ushi Oni

If you ever wander near the misty coasts or deep mountain lakes, beware of the Ushi Oni, the "ox demon." This savage monster has the horrific body of a giant spider (or occasionally a massive cat) topped with the horned head of an ox – although sometimes it flips into a human head on a beast’s body. It waits in the shadows of its watery lair, breathing out clouds of toxic poison, just waiting for you to step too close. More active Ushi Oni actively stalk the shorelines hunting for human flesh, while others strike deals with siren-like Yokai, who sing to draw people down to the water's edge. That's when the Ushi Oni strikes, killing you and sharing the feast with its siren friends. The most cruel ones will even haunt a single village night after night, devouring the people and leaving behind a trail of deadly disease and curses.

3. Umi Nyobo

Out in the dark ocean waves lives the Umi Nyobo, or "sea wife." They are the restless spirits of drowned women, and though they look like ordinary human women at a glance, a closer look reveals: scaly skin, webbed fingers, and rows of sharp shark-like teeth. They can easily overpower any human, they will drag fishermen down to feed on them, roam the coastlines to find unsuspecting victims, and even steal peoples children in the night. While they spend most of their time in the ocean, they can walk on land for up to a week at a time. They find immense, twisted pleasure in hunting humans on the shore, though every now and then, a piece of their old humanity surfaces, and they show unexpected kindness to a lucky soul.

4. Teke Teke

If you are walking down a lonely urban road at night and hear the sharp scratching patter of fingernails on the ground, then it’s already too late. That is the Teke Teke, named after the horrific noise she makes as she drags herself along the pavement. Teke Teke is a woman who is missing the entire lower half of her body. Running purely on her arms while wielding a sickle or a knife, she can crawl faster than a speeding car (climbing up to 95 mph). Once she catches you, she will ruthlessly rip or cut your body in half and then steal your legs. Absolutely nothing can save you. Dying by her hands dooms your spirit to become a Teke Teke too. They say that once you hear the origin story of the first Teke Teke, her legless spirit will find you exactly three days later, and your fate is sealed.

5. Obariyon

As you walk past the overgrown bushes on the side of a dark road, something small might suddenly leap out from the leaves, crying "Obariyon!” (which means "give me a piggyback ride)." This heavy little monster hitches a ride right on your back and shoulders. With every single step you take, the Obariyon grows heavier and heavier, all while viciously biting and chewing on your head. Most travelers never survive the journey; they either drop dead from pure exhaustion or are completely crushed to death under the impossible weight. However, if you do somehow manage to carry it all the way to your front door, the monster will vanish, leaving behind a sack of gold coins that will keep you rich for the rest of your days.

6. Jorogumo

If you are a young man walking through a busy city or a quiet town at night, beware of a beautiful woman offering promises of affection. She might actually be a Jorogumo, a name that translates to the "entangling bride". These cold-hearted monsters start their lives as ordinary golden orb weaver spiders, but after surviving for 400 years, they gain dark magic and develop a taste for human flesh, viewing people as nothing more than insects to be trapped. They are highly intelligent deceivers who shape-shift into gorgeous women to lure handsome men back to their nests which are located in caves, forests, or abandoned houses. Once you are trapped inside, the illusion shatters. Her massive spider legs and abdomen burst out, replacing her legs (or sometimes her whole body except for the head) and she tightly spins you into her web. She doesn't kill you quickly though. She will feast on you slowly over the course of the next few days while you die an agonizing death wrapped in silk. Jorogumo can even command lesser spiders and force them to spit fire, burning down the homes of anyone who dares to meddle in its affairs. Because they are so incredibly skilled at blending in, a Jorogumo can live right in the middle of a crowded city for years, stacking up a massive pile of prey without a single neighbor ever noticing.

7. Kotengu

High up in the desolate mountains, cliffs, and deep forests lives the Kotengu, the "lesser divine dog." These savage, solitary monsters are half-human and half-carnivorous bird, dressed in the robes of mystical hermits and wielding stolen weapons. They are chaotic creatures that go on blind, bloody frenzies when angered, destroying anything in their path. When they catch a human, they will brutally torture and murder them before devouring their flesh—unless you have a rare trinket to trade with them. They take a cruel joy in murder, torture, and suffering in general. Sometimes they will carry a person high above the treeline just to drop them to their death. Other times they tie screaming children to the highest parts of trees, where everyone can hear them but can never reach them. They even kidnap people and force-feed them feces until they completely lose their minds. Another delight of theirs is taking forcing their victims into blasphemy.

8. Kuro Bozu

The Koro Buzo, or "black monk," lives right in the midst of towns and villages. This shadowy, black skinned entity wears dark robes, has human and beast-like qualities, and carries a putrid stench of rotten fish and garbage. Its hands and feet are identical, allowing it to run at extreme speeds, it has a long grotesque tongue, and it can also rapidly alter its height. It waits until the house goes quiet, then sneaks into bedrooms and crouches over sleeping victims. Once in position, it presses close to steal and feast on your breath, all the while sticking its long, wet tongue deep into your mouth, nose, and ears. Those consistently haunted by the Koro Buzo eventually wake up incredibly sick, and this dark illness quickly spreads to the rest of the family. The only way to escape this rotting spirit is to abandon your home entirely and flee far away.

9. Tsurube Otoshi

Deep in the dense woods, hanging silently above walking paths, is the Tsurube Otoshi, translating to the "dropping well bucket." These yokai are giant, bodiless heads that watch the paths below from the high branches. When they are hungry and see someone, they drop straight down like a stone onto the unsuspecting victim, completely crushing and/or trapping them. They then laugh maniacally as they devour the trapped person or animal, before pulling themselves back up into the canopy, chanting and taunting the next traveler to try their luck. They love the thrill of the kill so much that they will drop down and crush people just for the fun of it, even when they aren't hungry. While the giant ones hunt alone, the smaller ones are known to hunt in vicious packs.

10. Tenome

The Tenome is a creature that prowls open fields and graveyards under the cover of night, driven by a horrific craving for fresh human bones. It takes the form of a blind old man, but if you look at the palms of its hands, you will see a pair of wide, staring eyes. Once you wander close enough for the Tenome to notice you, running is useless. It is incredibly fast and possesses a powerful sense of smell that can track your scent through the pitch-black night. When it catches you, it doesn't just bite—it presses its mouth against you and literally sucks your bones and internal organs straight out of your body, leaving nothing behind but a deflated, empty sack of skin.
